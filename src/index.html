    How to measure a pile

    1. Prepare. Use Chrome on your Android phone, in daylight or good lighting. The pile must stand free so you can walk all the way round it. Keep people and machines away from it while you walk.
    2. Optional, for the best accuracy: the tape check. Put two markers on flat ground near the pile — cones, spray-painted crosses or two sheets of paper — about 5–10 m apart, and measure the distance between their centres with a tape.
    3. Start. Choose the material, tap Start AR measurement and allow the camera. Move the phone gently left and right until the dots appear. Aim the centre dot at the middle of the pile and tap START. A yellow flag marks where you started.
    4. Walk one loop. Hold the phone upright at chest height and keep it pointed at the pile. Stay about 2–5 m from the foot of the pile (closer for small piles). Keep the whole height of the pile and a strip of ground in view. Walk slowly — about half your normal speed.
    5. Watch the dots. red = new detail, blue = position being worked out, green = fixed in 3D. The map at the top right fills in as the surface is measured; the ring shows how far round you are.
    6. Close the loop. When the ring is almost full the app asks you to slow down and points to the flag. Walk a few steps past it until you see Loop closed ✓, then tap FINISH.
    7. Tape check. During the walk, aim the centre dot at marker A and tap Mark A, then at marker B and tap Mark B. On the result page enter your taped distance.

    Tonnage and density

    Tonnage = volume × bulk density. The built-in densities are typical values only. Coal, ash and aggregates change with moisture and compaction, so calibrate once: measure a pile whose weight you know from the weighbridge, then use Calibrate density on the result page and save it as your own material.

    What affects accuracy

    • The phone's sense of distance. It can be off by 1–2%. Volume grows with length × width × height, so this becomes roughly 3–6% in volume. The tape check removes most of it.
    • Parts you did not see. The top of a tall pile, or a side you skipped, is filled in by estimation. Close the loop and keep the whole pile in view.
    • The ground under the pile. Photoscale assumes the ground under the pile continues at the level of the ground around it. A pile on a slope or in a pit is less certain.
    • Light and texture. Dark coal in poor light gives fewer dots. Daylight works best. Avoid rain, snow on the pile, and dust clouds.

    About

    Photoscale . It uses the phone's AR motion tracking (ARCore) for real-world scale, follows details in the camera image to place 3D points, adds ARCore depth, and builds a height map of the pile. The volume is the space between that surface and the ground level found around the pile. Nothing is uploaded.
